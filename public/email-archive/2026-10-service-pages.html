Why one "Services" page isn't enough

Lots of small business sites squeeze everything onto a single Services page: a list of bullet points, one line each. It looks tidy, but it quietly works against you.

When someone searches for exactly what you do, in their own words, Google looks for the page that best answers that search. A page that mentions twelve things in passing rarely beats a page that is properly about the one thing they asked for.

What tends to work better:

  1. A page for each service you want more work in. Not every service, just the ones that pay the bills.
  2. Say where you work. If you serve North Shore, Central Auckland or the wider region, say so in plain words on the page, rather than leaving people to guess.
  3. Add something only you can say. How the job runs, a typical timeframe, a real photo of your work, a question you get asked all the time. That's what turns a page from a list into a reason to call.
  4. Finish with one clear next step. Each page should end with a single, obvious way to get in touch.

A quick test: search Google for your main service plus your suburb or city, the way a customer would. Does your site show up? If it does, is it the page you'd actually want them to land on?
