On 22 November 2026, I started the process of claiming Kiwi Web Design in ChatGPT from New Zealand. It is early days, but the setup was more substantial than filling in a directory form: I had to find the business, claim it, show that I was authorised to manage it, and start an identity check.

This is a record of the path I saw, with screenshots from my own setup. It is not a promise that every New Zealand business will see the same options or be approved. OpenAI's product, eligibility checks and verification methods can change.

<div class="kwd-snippet">

**Can a New Zealand business claim a profile in ChatGPT?** Eligible businesses can claim an existing business or add one through OpenAI's Business Hub where ChatGPT and verification are supported. In my New Zealand setup, I was able to begin a claim for Kiwi Web Design and choose a verification route. You need to be the owner or an authorised representative, and OpenAI reviews claims and later changes.

</div>

OpenAI calls the wider feature [Businesses in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/business/businesses-in-chatgpt/). You may also see the management area described as Business Hub. People are already calling it a "ChatGPT Business Profile", which is useful shorthand, but it is worth knowing the official names when you look for current help.

## What I saw during the New Zealand setup

The process began with a business search screen, not a blank profile editor. I searched for Kiwi Web Design, then selected the option to create or claim the business.

![The Businesses in ChatGPT search screen used to find or add a business](/blog/chatgpt-business-profile-new-zealand-search.webp)

When the business record appeared, ChatGPT offered a claim screen that said I could manage the profile with my current account. This is an important checkpoint: use an account that the business owner controls and can retain access to.

![The ChatGPT business claim screen for Kiwi Website Design](/blog/chatgpt-business-profile-claim-new-zealand.webp)

## Step 1: Search for your business before creating a new one

Start by searching the business name, including the version customers are most likely to use. In my case, results included similarly named businesses, so I checked the name and address carefully before continuing.

That small check matters. Claiming the wrong record is not a shortcut to having a presence in ChatGPT. If the correct business is not listed, the flow may offer a way to add it instead.

OpenAI's [setup guide for businesses](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt) says eligible businesses can claim an existing business or add one where the feature and verification are available.

## Step 2: Claim the business with an account you control

After choosing the right listing, I was asked to claim it using my current ChatGPT account. Treat this as an ownership decision, not a task to hand to a casual contractor without a plan for access later.

For a small business, I would make sure the person completing the claim is either the owner or has written authority to manage the profile. OpenAI's guidance says the person claiming a business must be its owner or an authorised representative.

## Step 3: Prove that you can represent the business

The next screen gave me two ways to verify the claim: a code sent to the business phone number or a website-domain check. My dashboard showed the domain it expected me to control.

![ChatGPT offering business phone and website-domain verification options](/blog/chatgpt-business-profile-verification-options.webp)

The sensible choice depends on what you control today:

- Choose the business phone route only when the number belongs to the business and someone can receive the code.
- Choose the website-domain route when you can manage the site or DNS records and the domain belongs to the business.

Do not use an old agency phone number, an ex-staff member's address or a personal domain that is unrelated to the trading business. They can make the first verification feel convenient while creating an access problem later.

## Step 4: Complete the identity check

In my case, the dashboard then asked me to verify my identity with a government-issued ID before I could edit business information or publish a business agent. The process included photographing the front of the ID, waiting while it processed, then completing a camera check to confirm I was the person submitting it.

![ChatGPT requesting a clear photo of the front of a government-issued ID](/blog/chatgpt-business-profile-government-id.webp)

![ChatGPT's liveness check asking the claimant to centre their face and look left and right](/blog/chatgpt-business-profile-liveness-check.webp)

Use a secure device, follow the on-screen instructions, and do not share a government ID with anyone who does not need to handle it. Verification is not instant in every case. My screen showed an in-progress identity check, so I did not assume the profile was fully approved simply because I could see the dashboard.

## Step 5: Understand what the new dashboard does and does not mean

After the initial claim flow, I could see Kiwi Web Design in the Business Hub dashboard. It showed prompts to verify identity, review business-agent guidance and connect an ad account, along with Brand insights and Agent insights.

![Kiwi Web Design's early Business Hub dashboard with verification, guidance and insight prompts](/blog/chatgpt-business-hub-new-zealand-dashboard.webp)

At this early point, the mentions, profile views and website-click fields displayed dashes. That is normal for a brand-new setup with no reporting history; it is not a performance result.

The more important point is that seeing a dashboard does not mean ChatGPT will immediately recommend your business, send leads, or publish every change without review. OpenAI says it reviews claimed-business changes and may use other sources when presenting information. Keep your website, contact details and other public business information accurate as well.

## Do you need a ChatGPT Ads account to claim a business?

No. The optional "Connect an ad account" prompt appeared in my dashboard, but it was separate from the claim and verification steps. OpenAI's [business setup guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt) says an Ads account or an Enterprise agreement is not required just to claim an eligible business.

That distinction is useful for a small business. First, make sure the correct business information is claimed and accurate. Consider paid advertising separately, with a clear budget and a landing page that can answer a ready buyer's question.

## A business profile is not the same as a Business Agent

The dashboard I saw also referred to a business agent. That is a separate concept from claiming a business profile. OpenAI's launch information says Business Agents began with eligible ecommerce and online-service businesses in the United States, and agent eligibility has its own [policy requirements](https://openai.com/policies/business-agents-policy/).

In other words, do not promise a customer that claiming a profile automatically creates a chatbot, an agent, or a new sales channel. Start with the record you can verify, then check what options your own account shows.

## My pre-claim checklist for New Zealand small businesses

Before starting, I would have these ready:

1. The exact trading name, website and business phone number you want customers to see.
2. Access to the business phone or website domain needed for verification.
3. A ChatGPT account held by the owner or an authorised person with a clear access plan.
4. Government ID for the person completing identity verification, if the flow requests it.
5. A quick check that your website's contact details, services and location are current.
6. A decision about who will keep the profile up to date when the business changes phone number, address or website.

## The practical takeaway

For New Zealand small businesses, the first sensible goal is not to chase a new dashboard for its own sake. It is to make sure the right business is claimed by the right person and that the information people see elsewhere is consistent.

My own setup showed that the claim path can involve business-authority checks and identity verification, not just a few marketing fields. Set aside enough time to complete it properly, use an account the business controls, and take the review process seriously.
