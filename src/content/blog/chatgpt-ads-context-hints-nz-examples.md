<div class="kwd-snippet">

**A context hint adds truthful detail about what an offer does and when it is useful.** It is not an exact-match keyword, a guaranteed trigger or a way to override geography and policy. The worked examples below connect a buyer situation to a hint, an ad and a destination that can actually fulfil the promise.

</div>

*Sources checked 19 September 2026. All three businesses and campaigns below are illustrative. These are KWD-authored planning examples, not client quotations, approved ads or measured results. Use the proposed wording only where every offer detail is true.*

OpenAI recommends relevant, natural-language product information in its [context-hint guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001521-write-context-hints-for-chatgpt-ads). Our additional planning rule is to keep four things aligned: **buyer situation, truthful capability, destination evidence and measurable next step**. A clever hint cannot compensate for a missing capability.

## Example 1: A planned Auckland deck-cleaning job

**Assumed offer:** a local operator cleans residential decks, reviews the surface before recommending treatment and provides a booking enquiry path. The business has already checked offer eligibility and the available geography.

**Buyer situation:** a homeowner wants the outdoor area ready for visitors but is uncertain which cleaning approach suits the surface.

**Draft context hint:** "Residential deck cleaning for homeowners planning outdoor maintenance before hosting visitors. The operator reviews the deck material and condition before recommending a cleaning approach."

**Proposed ad headline:** "Plan Your Deck Clean"

**Proposed description:** "Tell us about your deck, location and preferred timing. We will review the job before recommending the next step."

**Destination brief:** show the actual service area, supported surfaces, preparation requirements, examples the operator has permission to use and the enquiry fields needed to assess the job. Do not advertise a treatment as safe for every surface without evidence.

**What we would evaluate:** whether enquiries are within coverage, concern a supported surface and contain enough information to arrange the work. A form completion from outside the service area is not an equally useful lead.

**What not to write:** "Show this ad only to Auckland homeowners this weekend." A hint is not a targeting or scheduling setting. Confirm those controls separately in the account.

## Example 2: An indoor family activity

**Assumed offer:** a venue publishes accurate age suitability, session availability and booking terms for an indoor activity.

**Buyer situation:** a parent is planning an outing and needs to balance weather, the children's ages and travel arrangements.

**Draft context hint:** "An indoor activity for families comparing outing options when outdoor plans depend on the weather. Published age guidance and session availability help parents decide whether a visit suits their family."

**Proposed ad headline:** "Find Your Next Family Outing"

**Proposed description:** "Check suitable ages, upcoming sessions and venue details before choosing your visit."

**Destination brief:** link to the activity and session information rather than a homepage carousel. Include the real location, supervision rules, accessibility information and cancellation terms. Only describe the experience as suitable for particular needs where the venue can substantiate that claim.

**What we would evaluate:** whether visitors find an appropriate session and complete a booking, alongside enquiries that expose missing suitability information. If people repeatedly ask whether toddlers can attend, improve that answer before broadening the campaign.

**What not to write:** "The best activity for every family." It replaces useful detail with an unsupported universal claim. The stronger message helps a family decide whether the offer is right for them.

## Example 3: Desk accessories for a compact workspace

**Assumed offer:** a NZ online shop sells desk organisers with reliable dimensions, stock information and delivery details.

**Buyer situation:** a person is fitting a work area into a shared room and wants to reduce clutter without buying unsuitable accessories.

**Draft context hint:** "Desk organisers for compact home workspaces. Product dimensions and compartment details help shoppers compare storage options for the desk space they have available."

**Proposed ad headline:** "Make Room on Your Desk"

**Proposed description:** "Compare organiser sizes and layouts, then check stock and NZ delivery details."

**Destination brief:** use a relevant collection or product page with dimensions visible before checkout, genuine product photography, stock status and delivery restrictions. Do not claim compatibility with every desk, monitor or device.

**What we would evaluate:** relevant product visits, completed purchases and avoidable compatibility questions or returns. A rise in clicks without suitable purchases may mean the ad promise is broader than the product range.

**What not to write:** "Only target shoppers in central Auckland." New product-feed campaigns currently have country-level targeting, according to OpenAI's [campaign setup documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001210-create-campaigns-for-chatgpt-ads). Writing a location instruction into a hint does not supply a missing control.

## A repeatable editing test

For each draft, identify every factual capability. Can the business fulfil it, and can the visitor verify it on the destination? Delete unsupported claims rather than burying qualifications in small print.

Next, remove instructions to the platform and keep information about the offer. If the remaining hint could apply equally to almost any business, add one truthful distinction: supported use, buying constraint, product detail or fulfilment condition. Specificity should come from the business, not an invented superlative.

Finally, name the business outcome before changing copy. Keep a record of what changed, when it changed and which destination was used. Changing the offer, creative and destination together may be necessary, but it makes it harder to explain what caused a difference in results.
