**Evidence-led answer:** Google Ads can produce measurable demand quickly when the campaign, offer and tracking are controlled. SEO can accumulate discoverability over a longer period, but Search Console clicks and GA4 sessions do not prove qualified leads or sales. The right first investment depends on urgency, unit economics, landing-page quality and what can be measured honestly.

</div>

Most SEO-versus-Ads articles compare generic advantages. This casebook uses two first-party New Zealand examples instead:

1. an API-verified Google Ads comparison for Northshore Assembly; and
2. organic-search and analytics evidence for one Kiwi Web Design resource page.

They are **not an A/B test** and should not be compared as if the businesses, periods and conversion definitions were the same. Their value is showing what each measurement system can and cannot answer.

## Case 1: rebuilding a Northshore Google Ads campaign

### Before: the Smart campaign

Google Ads API data for **1 March to 31 May 2026** recorded:

- $1,222.50 spend;
- 669 clicks;
- 107,363 impressions; and
- one primary tracked conversion.

The account also contained additional "all conversions" activity, which is why the comparison uses the primary `conversions` metric consistently rather than selecting a more flattering number.

### After: the focused Search campaign

For the exact 30-day period **24 June to 23 July 2026**, the rebuilt Search campaign recorded:

- $337.36 spend;
- 72 clicks;
- 675 impressions;
- 12 tracked enquiries; and
- $28.11 cost per tracked enquiry.

The campaign focused on high-intent furniture-assembly searches rather than relying on the previous Smart campaign's broad automated reach.

### What the data supports

It supports this statement: in those recorded periods, the focused Search campaign produced more primary tracked enquiries from less spend than the earlier Smart campaign.

### What the data does not support

The 12 tracked enquiries are not automatically 12 qualified prospects or 12 paying clients. This comparison does not provide sales revenue, close rate, margin or lifetime value. The periods were sequential, not randomised, and demand may have changed.

The campaign structure is therefore a strong operational signal, not proof that one change alone caused a particular business outcome.

## Case 2: a KWD resource accumulating organic visibility

The second case is KWD's website-cost resource and calculator. It is the site's strongest historical organic asset, so we use it as an example while keeping the page itself protected from this content programme.

Across **26 April 2025 to 26 August 2026**, first-party exports recorded:

- 359 Google Search Console clicks;
- 44,972 Search Console impressions;
- average Search Console position 10.4;
- 680 GA4 landing sessions; and
- 822 GA4 page views across historical title variants.

### What the data supports

The page earned sustained search visibility and brought users into the KWD site over a 16-month period. Its interactive estimator also answers a recurring buying question in a way generic commentary cannot.

### What the data does not support

Search Console clicks and GA4 sessions are measured differently and should not match. Page views include repeat viewing. The historical export does not provide a clean, page-specific set of qualified leads and sales, so we cannot calculate organic cost per acquisition or revenue from these numbers.

The data also does not prove that any single title, paragraph or calculator feature caused the performance. It is a useful content benchmark, not a licence to keep editing a page that already performs well.

## What Ads can teach SEO

A tightly controlled Search campaign can reveal which queries attract clicks, which offers produce enquiries and where irrelevant search terms consume budget. Those findings can improve service-page language and content priorities.

Ads data must still be interpreted carefully. A high-click keyword is not necessarily profitable, and Google Ads search-term data is not a complete market census.

## What SEO can do that Ads cannot

Useful organic pages can answer broad research questions, support brand trust and remain discoverable without paying for each visit. Google's guidance says advertising does not improve organic placement, and its AI search features continue to rely on core Search systems and crawlable web content.

That means a business preparing for AI-assisted search still needs the basics: specific service information, first-hand evidence, accessible text, clear authorship, internal links and accurate business details. See our [AEO and AI-search content guidance](/ai-seo-for-auckland-small-businesses/).

## A decision framework for an Auckland small business

### Start with Google Ads when:

- capacity exists now and enquiries are urgent;
- people already search for the service with commercial intent;
- the value of a qualified job can support the likely click cost;
- a focused landing page and conversion tracking are ready; and
- someone can review search terms and lead quality regularly.

### Start with SEO when:

- the business can invest for a longer evaluation period;
- customers need education and trust before enquiring;
- service, location and expertise content is currently thin;
- Google Business Profile and technical foundations need work; and
- the goal is to build an owned discovery asset.

### Fix the website first when:

- the offer is unclear;
- the page lacks proof or a usable next step;
- forms and phone actions are not tested;
- the business cannot distinguish traffic from qualified leads; or
- ownership and analytics access are uncertain.

Our review of six genuine website enquiries found that no buyer initially asked about tracking. That makes measurement a provider responsibility, not an optional extra requested by the customer.

## How to measure both channels without fooling yourself

Use the same outcome ladder for SEO and Ads:

1. Visibility: impressions and eligible reach.
2. Visits: clicks and landing sessions.
3. Actions: calls, forms, bookings and purchases.
4. Qualified leads: genuine prospects that fit the service.
5. Sales: accepted work and revenue.
6. Profit: revenue after delivery and acquisition costs.

Do not stop at the first metric that looks favourable. A channel can produce traffic without useful enquiries, or enquiries without profitable sales.
